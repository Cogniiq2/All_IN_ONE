Verdict

The engineering is genuinely good. It compiles clean, the design language is restrained and confident, the motion work is more sophisticated than most hospitality sites in Germany, and the SEO scaffolding is thorough.

But it is a beautiful website for a company that does not exist. It is branded "All in One Residences", sells five apartments described identically at 120 m² each, illustrates them with Pexels stock interiors, publishes six invented guest reviews and a 9.4/10 rating in machine-readable review markup, takes real card payments against no availability calendar, and ships an admin panel — with the family's invoices and business email behind it — publicly readable by anyone who types the URL.

None of that is fixed by better animation. The path to "the guest knows immediately they made the right decision" runs through truth first: real name, real apartments, real photographs, real availability, real reviews. Then design amplifies something that's actually there. That sequencing is the core recommendation of this report.

01

Launch blockers

Six things that must be resolved before the domain points at this site. Two of them are live risks right now if any version of this is already deployed.

F-01Critical

Your invoices and business email are publicly downloadable

public/admin/ is a compiled admin app served as a static site, and next.config.js redirects /admin straight to it. Its login is checked in browser JavaScript against a hardcoded password (a constant compiled into the bundle), so it can be read or simply bypassed. The bundle also contains the Supabase project URL and anon key in plain text.

That would be survivable if the database were locked down. It isn't: the migrations grant the anon role full select, insert, update and delete on invoices, emails and email_attachments, plus read access to the private invoices storage bucket. Anyone with the key — which is in a public file — can read every business email you've ingested, download every invoice PDF, or delete the lot. robots.txt doesn't even disallow /admin, so it's crawlable.

Fix Rotate the Supabase anon key today. Remove public/admin and the /admin redirect from this repo. Rewrite the RLS policies to require an authenticated user (Supabase Auth), never anon. Redeploy the admin as a separate, password-protected deployment on its own subdomain. This is unrelated to the guest-facing site and should not share a codebase with it.
F-02Critical

The booking flow charges real money against no availability data

booking-modal.tsx and sidebar-date-picker.tsx accept any future date. There is no availability source anywhere in the repo — no blocked dates, no existing reservations, no iCal feed from Booking.com or Airbnb. The modal then posts to n8n webhooks that create a live Stripe or PayPal checkout. A guest can pay in full, today, for a week that is already sold on Booking.com.

Pricing is equally thin: priceFrom × nights + €60. No seasonality, no Festspiele rate, no minimum stay, no weekly or monthly discount, no extra-guest fee, no VAT or Kurtaxe line on the total. Direct booking only beats the platforms if your rate management is at least as good as theirs.

Fix Decide the channel strategy first (see §08). If you stay on Booking and Airbnb, you need a channel manager — Smoobu, Beds24, Lodgify or Hostaway — as the single source of truth, with the website reading its availability and writing bookings back to it. Until that exists, the site should take enquiries and deposits, not instant confirmed payment. That change alone removes your largest operational risk.
F-03Critical

Invented reviews, published as structured data

"9.4 / 10 · 48+ Bewertungen" appears in the hero, the footer, the About page and every apartment page. lib/reviews.ts contains six named guest reviews. Worse, json-ld.tsx emits AggregateRating and three full Review objects with named authors and dates — machine-readable claims to Google that specific people wrote specific things.

In Germany this is misleading advertising under UWG §5 / §5b. Since 2022, §5b(3) explicitly requires disclosing how you verify reviews, and fabricated ones are expressly prohibited — enforceable by competitors and by Wettbewerbszentrale via Abmahnung. Google separately issues manual actions for fake review markup, which would cost you the rich snippets you built all this SEO for.

Fix Delete every invented review, rating number and rating schema block. Replace with real Booking.com/Airbnb ratings (quotable with attribution) or nothing at all. A new brand with no reviews and an honest "we're just opening — here's exactly what you get" reads far better than a suspiciously round 9.4 with no source.
F-04Critical

Third-party press and stock photography used without licence

public/images/richard-wagner-…-KD6MMB.jpg is an Alamy comp — a watermark-free preview, licensed for layout mock-ups only — and the journal page also hotlinks it directly from c8.alamy.com. The Festspielhaus photo on the homepage teaser has a filename matching a Bayerischer Rundfunk press image. Alamy actively pursues unlicensed comp use, and German Abmahnung costs for a single press photo commonly run four figures.

Fix Remove both immediately. Licence properly, use Wikimedia images with a compatible licence and correct attribution, or shoot Bayreuth yourself — a walk with a good camera on a clear morning solves it permanently and looks more like you.
F-05Critical

Impressum, Datenschutz and AGB are placeholder text

The Impressum lists "Musterstraße 1" and "USt-IdNr: DE XXX XXX XXX", names no GmbH, no Handelsregister number, no Registergericht, and no Geschäftsführer in the form §5 DDG requires. The privacy policy is 63 lines and names none of your actual processors — Stripe, PayPal, n8n (n8n.cogniiq.co), Supabase, Netlify or Google Fonts. The AGB is 72 lines for a business that takes 100% prepayment from consumers.

A missing or wrong Impressum is directly abmahnfähig in Germany, and it is the single cheapest thing on this list to get right.

Fix Give me the company data in §08.A and I'll write all three properly. Have your Steuerberater or a Fachanwalt für IT-Recht review the AGB before launch — especially the cancellation terms and the consumer Widerrufsrecht carve-out for accommodation (§312g(2) Nr. 9 BGB), which the current AGB never mentions.
F-06Critical

The inventory doesn't exist

The site sells five named apartments — Maison Sternplatz, Loge am Sternplatz, Atelier Opernstraße, Penthouse Belvédère, Design Loft Innenstadt — each "ca. 120 m²", each sleeping exactly 4, each with an identical amenity list and a garage. You have two lettable apartments in Schulstraße and one in Opernstraße still being renovated.

The data is internally inconsistent too: residences.ts files Atelier Opernstraße under the "Sternplatz Collection"; the same apartment is "Penthouse Belvédère" in one file and "Belvédère Schulstraße" in another; walking times to the Hofgarten are 3–5 minutes in German and 10 minutes in English; the FAQ says the Festspielhaus is 15 minutes by car while the apartment pages imply walking distance.

Fix Rebuild lib/residences.ts from the real property data in §08.B. Two apartments presented in full, honest depth will convert far better than five described identically. The "collections" framing is a device that only makes sense at scale — with two units it reads as padding.
02

The BoLaGio rebrand

"All in One" is a generic name that says nothing. BoLaGio is the opposite — it already carries the whole story, and almost nobody realises it. That's the most valuable asset in this project and the site currently doesn't use it at all.

Scope of the rename

  • Every visible string — 141 occurrences of the old naming across components, plus lib/translations.ts, all metadata, all JSON-LD, sitemap, robots.
  • The logo — brand-logo.tsx hand-draws an "All in" calligraphic monogram in SVG. It has to be replaced outright, not recoloured. public/images/logo-allinone.png is a 0-byte file.
  • Domain, email and schema — allinone-residences.de is hardcoded in ~20 places including metadataBase, canonicals and hreflang.
  • The repository itself — All_IN_ONE, and the aio- prefixes in localStorage keys.

What I'd build the identity on

Bogdana, Lazar, Djordje. Three names, three initials, and — once Opernstraße is finished — three residences. That correspondence is the design system: B · L · G as a monogram that resolves into the wordmark, one letter per apartment, each apartment carrying its own letter as a quiet mark on its page. It's structural, it's true, and no competitor can copy it because it's your children's names.

It also solves the "why book direct" problem emotionally rather than commercially. Booking.com cannot offer a family. A site that says this house is named after our three children, and one of us answers the phone is doing something the platforms structurally cannot.

F-07High

The children are currently listed as staff with job titles

app/about/about-client.tsx presents Bogdana as "Standards & Qualität", Lazar as "Hospitality-Projekte" and Djordje as "Operations & Entwicklung", with professional biographies. If any of them are minors, publishing a named role and bio for them on a commercial site is both a data-protection problem under DSGVO Art. 8 and, frankly, a tonal one — it reads as invented corporate structure, which undercuts the honesty the page is trying to establish.

Fix Tell me their ages. If minors: name the company for them, tell the story of the name, and keep the operating team to the adults who actually run it. That's warmer and safer than fictional job titles.
F-08Medium

The Niš café and the "coming soon" pages have no strategy

There are two unfinished pages — /coming-soon/gelateria-michele and /coming-soon/cafetaria-bayreuth — with email capture forms that submit nowhere, linked from the footer but excluded in robots.ts. Meanwhile the café you actually own, in Niš, appears nowhere.

Fix Decide whether BoLaGio is a Bayreuth lettings brand or a family holding brand. If the latter, the Niš café belongs on the About page as a second chapter — it makes the family real and international. If those two coming-soon pages aren't happening, delete them; half-finished pages read as neglect.

04

Design & the feel gap

You asked for the visitor to know immediately they made the right decision. The current site is handsome but anonymous — it is correctly executed luxury rather than this particular family's house in this particular city. Here's precisely where the gap sits.

F-09High

The hero is a stock photo of nowhere

The single most consequential image on the site — full-bleed, first paint, behind the headline "Fünf Apartments. Mitten in Bayreuth." — is a generic interior. The vertical side labels say "Deutschland / Bayern". There is nothing in the first screen that could only be BoLaGio, and nothing that could only be Bayreuth.

Fix This is a photography problem, not a code problem, and it's where I'd spend money first. A half-day with an interiors photographer — blue hour with the lamps on, morning light across the floor, one detail shot per room, one shot of the actual street door — is worth more to your conversion rate than every animation in the repo combined.
F-10High

Eleven homepage sections, six of which say the same thing

home-client.tsx stacks Hero → ProofStrip → CollectionsTeaser → WhyChoose → Pillars → ResidencesPreview → DirectBooking → Reviews → BayreuthTeaser → TrustTeaser → FinalCta. ProofStrip, WhyChoose, Pillars, DirectBooking, TrustTeaser and FinalCta are all variations on "trust us, book direct". Restraint is the actual signal of luxury; repetition reads as insecurity.

Fix Cut to six: Hero → The two apartments (in real depth) → Why direct (once, with concrete numbers) → Bayreuth & the Festspiele → The family → Book. Each section then gets room to be excellent instead of adequate.
F-11High

Micro-typography has crossed from refined into unreadable

Labels run at 7px to 9px with 0.28–0.42em letter-spacing, in colours like text-white/20, white/22 and white/34 over a photograph. The hero subline is text-white/46. Much of this fails WCAG AA contrast outright, and 7px uppercase is genuinely illegible on a phone in daylight — which is where most of your Festspiele guests will first see it.

Fix Floor of 11px for any real text, 10px for decorative eyebrows, and lift every muted opacity to a minimum 4.5:1 against its actual background. Precision at legible sizes reads as expensive; unreadable small type reads as a template.
F-12Medium

Motion is scattered rather than orchestrated

There's a cursor glow, a pulsing gold orb, a grid overlay, parallax, word-by-word reveals, magnetic nav items, shimmer sweeps on three different buttons, marquees, float animations and a scroll progress bar — plus 30 keyframe definitions in globals.css, many unused. Individually each is well made. Together they compete.

Fix Pick one signature moment and make it exceptional — my recommendation: on load, the B·L·G monogram resolves into the BoLaGio wordmark while the hero image completes a slow crop-out. Everything else drops to a single restrained reveal on scroll. One remembered moment beats twelve forgettable ones.
F-13Medium

The "why book direct" argument is asserted, never shown

The strongest line in the whole repo is buried in a FAQ answer on /book-direct: platform fees typically add 10–20%. That's your entire commercial thesis and it's in an accordion.

Fix Put it on the homepage as an actual comparison, with your real numbers — the same apartment, the same week, the platform total beside the direct total, and the difference named. Then add benefits the platforms can't match: guaranteed garage space, late checkout, a welcome basket, direct WhatsApp to the family. Concrete beats adjectival.
F-14Medium

English exists in the UI but not in search

The language toggle swaps strings client-side and stores the choice in localStorage. There are no /en routes, so no English page is indexable, and the hreflang tags in layout.tsx point de-DE, en-US and x-default at the identical URL. For a Wagner festival that draws visitors from the UK, US and Japan, that's the most valuable audience you have, invisible to Google.

Fix Move to Next.js locale routing with real /de and /en paths and correct hreflang. Worth doing before you invest further in SEO copy.
05

Craft & performance

Detail work. Individually small; collectively the difference between "nice site" and "immaculate".

F-15High

Desktop navigation triggers a full page reload

navbar.tsx renders desktop links as <motion.a href> instead of Next's <Link>. Every click on Residences, About or Contact reloads the entire application — white flash, fonts re-render, hero animation replays. The mobile menu uses Link correctly, so desktop feels notably worse than mobile. This is the most immediately noticeable "cheap" moment on the site.

FixWrap the magnetic effect around <Link>. Small change, disproportionate improvement.
F-16High

No favicon, no share image, no logo file

There is no app/icon, no favicon.ico, no opengraph-image. logo-allinone.png is zero bytes, and the JSON-LD points at /logo.png, which doesn't exist. Open Graph falls back to a Pexels URL. Every WhatsApp share — your most likely referral channel — shows a stock photo and a blank browser tab.

FixShip a favicon set, an Apple touch icon, a real logo asset, and a designed 1200×630 OG image per key page.
F-17Medium

Image optimisation is switched off, and assets are duplicated

next.config.js sets images.unoptimized: true, so no WebP/AVIF conversion and no responsive sizing. public/images/ is 5.5 MB, and almost every file exists twice — one 767 KB PNG is present in five identical copies ("copy copy copy copy"). Real apartment photography will multiply this.

FixRe-enable optimisation (Netlify's Next plugin supports it), delete the duplicates, and set explicit sizes on every <Image>.
F-18Medium

Scroll handlers re-render the hero on every frame

hero-section.tsx stores window.scrollY in React state on every scroll event, re-rendering the whole hero — background, overlays, orb, headline, stat chips — continuously while scrolling. On a mid-range Android this will stutter, which is exactly the opposite of the impression you're paying for.

FixUse Framer Motion's useScroll + useTransform so the parallax runs off the compositor and never re-renders the tree.
F-19Medium

The booking modal isn't accessible

It's a plain fixed <div>: no role="dialog", no aria-modal, no focus trap, no Escape-to-close, and the page behind it keeps scrolling. Keyboard users can tab out of the modal into the page underneath mid-payment. Radix Dialog is already a dependency and used elsewhere.

FixRebuild on Radix Dialog. Same visuals, correct behaviour, less code.
F-20Medium

Payment payloads are logged to the browser console

booking-modal.tsx calls console.log with the full payload — guest name, email, phone, notes, total — plus the webhook response. It's client-side, so it's the guest's own data, but it's noise in production and it exposes your n8n response shape to anyone inspecting.

FixStrip the logs. While you're there: the "Nur anfragen" fallback button sets a success state locally without ever sending the request anywhere — those enquiries are silently discarded.
F-21Medium

No analytics, so no way to know whether any of this works

Nothing measures traffic, funnel drop-off or direct-vs-platform mix. You're about to make a commercial bet on direct booking with no instrument on the dashboard.

FixAdd Plausible or Fathom — cookieless, so no consent banner is required in Germany, which also keeps the site clean. Track: apartment page views, date-picker opens, checkout starts, completions.
06

German legal exposure

Beyond the placeholder legal pages (F-05), three specific items apply to a German short-term letting business and are currently unaddressed.

F-22High

Google Fonts loaded from Google's servers

globals.css opens with an @import from fonts.googleapis.com, which transmits every visitor's IP address to Google before any consent. This is the exact configuration the Landgericht München I ruled against in 2022 (3 O 17493/20), and it spawned a wave of Abmahnungen against German sites. Ironically the repo already self-hosts one font correctly via next/font.

FixMove Playfair, Cormorant and Inter to next/font/google, which downloads and self-hosts them at build time. Faster too — it removes a render-blocking third-party request.
F-23Medium

No Meldeschein flow, no Gästebeitrag handling

German law (BMG §29/§30) requires accommodation providers to collect and retain a registration form from every guest. There's no digital check-in flow, and no line item for any Bayreuth Gästebeitrag/Kurtaxe in the price breakdown. Both belong in the booking flow — and a well-designed digital Meldeschein is a genuine quality signal, not just compliance.

FixConfirm Bayreuth's current position on both (see §08.A), then build a post-booking guest form that collects the Meldeschein data and issues the door code on completion.
F-24Medium

Invoicing and VAT are unmodelled

/book-direct promises "eine vollständige Rechnung mit USt-ID" for every booking, but nothing issues invoices, and the total is a flat sum with no tax breakdown. German short-term accommodation is 7% VAT while ancillary services — cleaning fees can fall here — are 19%. Your €60 cleaning fee may need to be split out and taxed separately.

FixGet your Steuerberater's ruling on the cleaning-fee split, then model VAT explicitly in the price breakdown and generate a compliant invoice automatically on payment.
07

What to keep

This isn't a rewrite. A great deal here is worth building on, and I want to be explicit about it so nothing good gets thrown out with the rebrand.

  • The technical foundation is sound. tsc --noEmit passes clean, the production build succeeds, 33 pages render fully static, and shared JS is 86 kB — genuinely lean for a site with this much motion.
  • The design language is a good starting point. Champagne-on-ink, a 2px border radius, Playfair against Inter, the film-grain overlay, and the hairline gold rules form a coherent system. It needs rooting in Bayreuth and in your family, not replacing.
  • The SEO scaffolding is unusually thorough — sitemap, robots, breadcrumb/Event/FAQ schema, and five long-form German journal articles targeting real Festspiele search intent. Once the review markup is removed, this is a real asset.
  • The Festspiele 2026 focus is commercially right. That's your highest-margin window and the site already anticipates it.
  • The bilingual content layer exists — every string is already written in German and English. It only needs routing.
  • The apartment detail page is well structured — gallery, highlights, walking distances, sticky booking sidebar, per-apartment FAQ. The skeleton is right; it needs true content.

09

Proposed sequence

I'd work in three passes. The first needs almost nothing from you and removes the real risk; the second is where the site becomes BoLaGio; the third is where it becomes exceptional.

Pass 1

Make it safe

Rotate the Supabase key and lock down RLS. Remove the public admin build. Delete the invented reviews and the rating schema. Remove the Alamy and press images. Self-host the fonts. Strip the console logging and fix the silently-discarded enquiry button. None of this needs a single answer from you — I can start today.

Pass 2
